GPU Benchmark Report

RTX PRO 6000 Max-Q (96 GB) vs RTX PRO 5000 (72 GB) — 17 shared models, 4 exclusive, isolated single-GPU containers, Ollama 0.40.6

Why I ran this

I had both NVIDIA Blackwell workstation cards in my home lab simultaneously and wanted real numbers before committing. After 38 benchmark runs across 21 models — 8B to 480B — I kept the RTX PRO 6000 Max-Q and passed the PRO 5000 to a friend. The deciding factor: at the 70B tier the PRO 5000 hit 97% VRAM utilisation and fell to 5.9 tok/s; the 6000's 24 GB headroom kept the same model at 27.6 tok/s — nearly 5× — and it's the only option that fits qwen3:235b (142 GB) entirely in VRAM. For ≤32B models the gap is only 10–20%, so the 5000 is a solid card; it just couldn't hold the 70B reasoning models I actually use.

Benchmark Architecture

Each GPU ran in its own isolated container with exclusive PCI passthrough — no resource contention. Models stored on a shared volume so every model was available to both without re-downloading.

deepseek-r1:70b — the decision point: 5.9 vs 27.6 tok/s on the PRO 5000 vs 6000. The 5000 hit 97% VRAM utilisation (71 / 73.4 GB), forcing the KV cache into system RAM. The 6000's 24 GB headroom ran the same model fully in VRAM at nearly 5× the speed.

Final Setup — Three Ollama Lanes on 96 GB

After benchmarking I kept the PRO 6000 and configured three independent Ollama instances on the same card, each serving a different client group. Because they all share one pool of VRAM, what matters is the total resident weight, not the per-lane limit.

Why three queues, not one? Ollama processes one forward pass at a time per instance. Three instances give three independent queues — an 8-second 70B generation on the agents lane doesn't block the IDE extension waiting on :11436. NUM_PARALLEL=4 per lane adds continuous batching up to 4 in-flight requests per queue; 3 × 4 = 12 theoretical concurrent slots, all VRAM-permitting.
