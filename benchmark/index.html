GPU Benchmark Report

RTX PRO 6000 Max-Q (96 GB) vs RTX PRO 5000 (72 GB) — 17 shared models, 4 exclusive, isolated single-GPU containers, Ollama 0.40.6 · plus pooled 6000 + 5000 (168 GB) and 5000 + 3090 (96 GB)

Why I ran this — and where it landed

I had both NVIDIA Blackwell workstation cards in my home lab at the same time and wanted real numbers before committing. The first pass (38 runs, 21 models, 8B to 480B, default settings) favoured the 6000: at the 70B tier the PRO 5000 hit 97% VRAM and fell to 5.9 tok/s while the 6000 held 27.6 tok/s.

A second, tuned pass changed the picture. With flash attention, a q8_0 KV cache and a sensible context, the same 70B model runs at 25.0 tok/s on the 5000 vs 28.2 on the 6000: the earlier gap was a KV cache spilling out of VRAM, not the card. Across the same models the 6000 is about +16% faster, roughly what its extra memory bandwidth predicts.

Decision: I'm returning the RTX PRO 6000. A second PRO 5000 is on order, for 2 × 72 GB = 144 GB. The alternative is to keep running the 5000 with an RTX 3090 (96 GB pooled, measured below) until the 5500 ships.

Benchmark Architecture

Each GPU ran in its own isolated container with exclusive PCI passthrough — no resource contention. Models stored on a shared volume so every model was available to both without re-downloading.

Initial Setup — Two RTX 3090s (48 GB)

Where this started: two 24 GB RTX 3090s in one server and three Ollama lanes. The largest coding model was split by layers across both cards, which pools VRAM but does not make a single request faster: the cards take turns on every token.

Tested Setup — Three Ollama Lanes on the 6000 (96 GB)

While the PRO 6000 was in, I ran three independent Ollama instances on the one card, each serving a different client group. Because they all share one pool of VRAM, what matters is the total resident weight, not the per-lane limit. The same lane layout carries over to the PRO 5000s.

Why three queues, not one? Ollama processes one forward pass at a time per instance. Three instances give three independent queues — an 8-second 70B generation on the agents lane doesn't block the IDE extension waiting on :11436. NUM_PARALLEL=2 per lane gives 2 concurrent KV cache slots per queue; 3 × 2 = 6 total KV slots across all lanes, all VRAM-permitting. (NUM_PARALLEL was briefly set to 4 during testing but caused VRAM saturation at 96.4/97.9 GB on the 96 GB card — the pre-allocated KV headroom alone exceeded available VRAM with two large models resident.)

6000 + 5000: pooling only pays when a model does not fit one card. Ollama keeps a model on a single card when it fits, so 25 pooled runs landed at ×1.00 of the 6000 alone (range ×0.95–×1.03). When a model really was split across both cards it ran at ×0.87–×1.04 of the 6000 alone: splitting costs little, spilling to system RAM costs a lot. The 150 GB qwen3-coder:480b IQ1_M ran only here, at 57.8 tok/s, against 9.9 tok/s on the 6000 alone when ~53 GB spilled to RAM.
5000 + 3090: a 24 GB overflow, with a speed tax. It runs 72–90 GB models fully on GPU (qwen2.5:72b Q8 at 13.8 tok/s) and rescues untuned big models (Scout ×1.8, deepseek-r1:70b ×3.3 vs the 5000 alone). But Ollama also puts part of models that would fit the 5000 onto the slower 3090 (936 GB/s vs 1,344 GB/s), and that share costs 5–20% (gpt-oss:120b 149 → 133 tok/s). Small models stay on the 5000 and the 3090 idles.
Thermals and power on 5000 + 3090. 10 minutes of back-to-back requests: 207 → 208 tok/s, 0 of 120 requests throttled, the 5000 peaked at 90 °C then settled at 88 °C. Capped at 250 W: −4% speed (208 → 199 tok/s), −48 W, fan 83% → 69%. The 3090 logged brief thermal slowdown at a 63 °C core, which points at its GDDR6X memory. Case airflow is the fix, not a blower 3090.
2× PRO 5000 (144 GB), untested: each model stays on one card when it fits, so it runs at PRO 5000 speed. 72–140 GB models split across two equal 1.34 TB/s cards, with no slow card for the fast one to wait on, so it should split at least as well as 6000 + 5000 did (×0.87–×1.04). The one thing it can't hold is the 150 GB 480B IQ1_M. I'll rerun the same harness when the second card arrives.
