Claude privacy and ownership: a plain guide

What happens to your chats and your code on a personal Claude plan, which switches matter, and what I checked for hidden tracking. Written for anyone, no technical background needed.

Unofficial, not legal advice. This is one user's reading of the public terms as of 2026-11-07. Terms change. For anything that matters, read the official consumer terms and privacy policy, or ask a lawyer.

The short version

  • One switch decides most of it. In Settings, Privacy, there is a toggle that lets your chats be used to improve the models. Off means shorter retention and no training use. On means longer retention (the terms describe up to five years) and use for training.
  • Your content stays yours. The terms say you keep ownership of what you put in and, as between you and Anthropic, of what you get out. Check the current wording yourself.
  • Your own computer keeps a copy too. Coding tools save session transcripts locally, and that copy follows your computer's settings, not the cloud ones.
  • No hidden watermarks or GUIDs were detected in the generated code I checked (details and limits below).

1. Where your data lives

2. The privacy switch

In Settings, Privacy, look for the option about helping improve Claude with your chats.

  • Off: chats are kept for a short window (the terms describe about 30 days) and are not used for training.
  • On: chats can be used for training and kept much longer (the terms describe up to five years).

Flagged or policy-violating conversations may be kept longer under either setting. Turning it off later does not recall anything already used.

3. Who owns the work

The consumer terms say you keep your rights in what you submit, and that the outputs are yours as between you and Anthropic. They do not say you get a guaranteed copyright: whether AI-assisted output is copyrightable depends on your country and on how much human authorship went in. If the work is part of a job, your employment agreement usually decides who owns it, not the AI tool.

4. Attribution lines in commits

Some coding tools add a line such as "Co-Authored-By" to commit messages or a "generated with" note to pull requests. That is plain visible text, not a hidden mark, and it can be turned off in the tool's settings (an attribution setting with empty values). Whether to show it is your choice and your workplace's policy.

5. Hidden watermarks and tracking IDs: what I checked

No hidden watermarks or GUID-shaped identifiers were detected in the code I scanned.

  • I scanned two sets of generated files (89 and 82 files) for zero-width and other invisible characters, unusual whitespace patterns, and GUID/UUID-shaped strings that did not belong to the code.
  • Findings: nothing hidden. Any identifiers present were ordinary ones the code itself needed.

Limits of this check.

  • It can only find marks that look like the things I searched for. A subtle statistical watermark in word choice would not show up this way.
  • A clean scan on my files is not proof for yours. It is a method you can repeat on your own files.
  • I cannot see what happens on the provider's servers. For that, the terms and privacy policy are the source.

6. Removing your history

  • Cloud: delete individual chats in the app. Deleted chats leave your history right away and are removed from backend storage on the schedule in the terms.
  • On your computer: coding-tool transcripts are plain files in your home folder. They are cleaned up automatically after a set number of days (a setting you can shorten), or you can delete them yourself.
  • Git history: removing a line from an old commit means rewriting history, which changes every later commit. Do it on your own unshared branches, keep a backup, and never rewrite history others depend on.

Checklist

  1. Decide on the training toggle and set it deliberately.
  2. Read the current terms for ownership wording that applies to you.
  3. If work belongs to an employer or client, follow their policy on AI tools first.
  4. Choose whether commits show attribution lines.
  5. Set how long local transcripts are kept.
